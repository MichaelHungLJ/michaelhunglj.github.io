Have you ever been in a situation where you used up all your brain cells trying to figure out every possible option, listing the pros and cons, obsessing over what if scenarios, only to end up in analysis paralysis, mentally drained, and ultimately making a sub-optimal decision? You’re overthinking.

<div align="center">
I believe everyone overthinks, but not everyone is an <i>overthinker</i>.
</div>

&nbsp;

---
&nbsp;

### How I struggle

Throughout my life, I’ve often found myself trapped in my own head, whether it’s worrying about life direction, processing difficult emotions, or trying to understand what I really want. Overthinking has been detrimental to both my mental health and social life.

I think the reason many of us overthink is because we’re searching for a perfect answer, or at least one that lets us feel at peace. We want certainty before we act. But life rarely offers that kind of clarity.

There’s nothing wrong with overthinking. Sometimes we’re faced with problems that require deeper thought such as reflecting, making life choices, or planning for the future. It’s crucial that we invest time to weigh our options. But the problem lies in overthinking for long periods of time. We burn out fast, mentally and emotionally drained, giving ourselves added pressure and stress.

People around me like my family, friends, and colleagues have told me that I think too much. And they’re right. Sometimes, I wish there were a switch in my head I could just flip off.

Trying to “stop thinking” doesn’t really work either. Our minds don’t simply let go of a thought. We just shove it to the back of our heads and let it quietly smolder. But it’s still there, slowly draining us over days, weeks, even months.

I’ve been experimenting with ways to stop overthinking.

&nbsp;

---
&nbsp;

### Ways I try to manage


**“Just act. Don’t think.”**  
It sounds extreme and dumb, and it is. I would tell myself to stop over analyzing and just go with my gut. But looking back, I realized that approach sometimes caused problems or unnecessary tension, especially in social settings. I wasn’t thinking about how my actions might affect others. I was treating everything as trivial, just to avoid the mental weight.

Another approach was to only overthink the important things.  
For trivial matters, like what to eat, where to go, or group plans, I’d just go with the flow, letting others decide. This way, I could preserve my mental battery. But that also backfired in some ways. I feel that I'm perceived as indecisive, passive, or uninterested.

Even so, when it comes to deeper problems, I still overthink a lot. And it drains me. It feels like my brain is stuck in a loop. During conversations, I often find myself zoning out because the thoughts in my mind are louder than the world around me.

**Overthinking is like being stuck in a realm of _what if_, _what should_, _what could_, and _what has_, but never _what is_.**  
You become so disconnected from the present that it’s hard to engage and connect with others. You’re physically there, but mentally elsewhere.

### Where I am now

The truth is, I’ve accepted that overthinking is part of my nature. Instead of stop trying to overthink, I’m constantly trying to find ways to better manage it. Maybe the key to overthinking is learning when to let go, have a little faith, and live in the present.

So if you’re like me, someone whose mind tends to wander a little too far, maybe this post is for you.
